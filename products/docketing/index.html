02 · Every module, in detail

What each one holds, what you do in it, and what follows.

The modules work in four groups that share one book. Each group below is working in the docket today.

Group 01 · The book
01–07

The book

Matter opening, by hand or from the papers; Document Drop; case files, the matter drawer, hearing updates and documents.

A matter opens once, and now it can open itself. Drop the file's PDFs and the papers are read: the case type and sub-type, the register fields, the cause title, the client, the next date. A bound court file is split into the documents inside it and each is filed where it belongs. From then on, anything that arrives is dropped on the app and docketed to the right file and folder, the file is worked in the drawer, and kept current from the hearing itself.

Modules in this group
  • Matter Opening. Case type and sub-type, parties with their designations and the cause title flipped when the client defends, forum, originator, partner and team leader, subject value, the first diary entry and next action date. Fields come from the case type; nothing is hard-coded.
  • New Matter from Documents. Drop the file's PDFs into the new-matter drawer. Each is read page by page, scanned copies included. One pass returns the file type and sub-type, the register fields for that class, the cause title, the client matched against the client book, the next date and action if the papers show one, and a label for every document. A bound bundle is cut into the FIR, each order, the petition with its annexures, each filed into the folder its kind points to, with the bundle as uploaded kept in Complete File. The form is filled for your review; the team and the first diary entry stay yours; nothing exists until you click Create.
  • Document Drop. The button in the top bar, or simply dropping files anywhere in the app. PDF, Word and Excel are read, and one decision names the file on the register it belongs to, the folder in that file's plan, a retrieval name, and the reason. You see each proposal with its confidence, change the file, folder or name, or untick a document, and only then is it docketed.
  • Case Files. Tabs per case type, folders within them, filters by status, fee earner and forum, search across the book, and a sortable table or a card layout for the same list.
  • Matter Drawer. Particulars, order sheet, documents and tasks in a drawer over the list, so reading a file never costs you your place. A Research button carries the whole file into the law library.
  • Hearing Updates & Order Sheet. Record the hearing update and the next date of hearing from the drawer; the order sheet grows, and the dashboard, calendar and cause list follow without re-entry.
  • Documents & E-files. Folder templates per case type and sub-type (pleadings, applications and interim, affidavits of evidence, bail and custody, award record and more), uploads accepted as PDF, Word and Excel, scanned pages transcribed once and cached by content, and a file room that counts documents by type and lists the recently filed.

Group 02 · Dates
08–11

Dates

Dashboard, calendar, cause list, and tasks and limitation.

Every hearing date and diary entry recorded on a file appears in four places at once: the dashboard that shows the office where it stands, the calendar that shows you your month, the board that shows what is listed on a given day, and the task list that shows what is overdue and by how many days. Nothing is entered twice.

Modules in this group
  • Dashboard. Listed today, overdue actions, dates this week and matters reserved for orders across the office; next dates sorted by hearing date with overdue first; matters by case type; status of the whole book including disposed; recent activity; and the number of listed dates per week over the next eight.
  • Calendar. A personal month diary: hearing chips and open diary entries on the files where you are partner or team leader, with the month at a glance as a list beside it.
  • Cause List. The board for the day, or any chosen day, from the hearing dates on the docket, grouped by court, with tomorrow ready beside it. Your matters, or the whole firm's board for the admin.
  • Tasks & Limitation. Diary entries as work in hand, sorted into overdue, today, this week and later, by fee earner, each carrying its due date and how many days past it.

Group 03 · Intelligence
12, 15–16

Intelligence

The docket copilot, live case status from the court, and reports. Research has its own section below.

A docket that only records is a diary. This one answers. The copilot is handed the whole live book, minus document bytes, on every question, so it reasons about your files as they stand today. Case status is read from the court and tied to the file number. Reports say where the book actually sits, not where the timesheets say it does.

Modules in this group
  • Docket Copilot. Ask anything about the docket in plain language. The digest of the live workspace is rebuilt for each question, answers stream back, and the conversation keeps running whichever screen you move to.
  • Check Case Status. Case type, number and year against the Delhi High Court's records, live. Results are curated to the fields that matter, tied to the file number in the book, and the lookup keeps polling in the background until it returns.
  • Reports & Analytics. Workload by fee earner, counting the live matters where they lead; forum distribution; the top eight stages across live matters; adjournment rate; median age; and matters disposed over the last 24 months.

Group 03 · Research
13–14

Research

Case-law chat over the Supreme Court and all 25 High Courts, and a research memo built from a file's own papers.

The law library lives with the docket. Judgments from the Supreme Court and every High Court, published as open data, are downloaded and indexed on the firm's own machine, court by court, as you choose them. You ask in plain language; the answer cites the judgments it relies on, and every citation links to the source PDF in the court's own records. Attach a file from the docket and the same answer is applied to that file's facts.

Modules in this group
  • Research. Full-text search across judgment text and titles, grouped per case, with the reading of the judgment itself when the question needs it. Multiple sessions with history. Each question runs on the server, so an answer keeps streaming while you switch tabs, open another session, or reload.
  • The library. Research → Library lists the 26 courts. Tick a court and it is fetched once from the public mirror and indexed in the background, resuming if interrupted. The Supreme Court is the largest at about 3.8 GB; the Delhi High Court about 2 GB.
  • A file from the docket. Attach one from the composer, or press Research in the file's drawer. Its particulars, order sheet and the text of its documents go with every question, so authorities are applied to the file's own facts. Scanned documents are transcribed once and cached.
  • Research Memo from a File. With a file attached, one button frames the research propositions from the papers and researches each of them: facts in brief, a section per proposition with authorities and their application, an overall assessment, and the cases relied on.
  • Cost on every answer. Each research answer shows its estimated cost, and earlier turns of a session are served from cache rather than re-read.

Group 04 · The firm
18–23

The firm

Clients, team chat, notifications, users and permissions, firm settings and workspaces, and AI usage.

The book belongs to the firm, and the firm is more than its files. Clients are kept as a book of their own, the conversation about a matter happens next to the matter, every action is announced to whoever needs to see it, and what each person may change is decided by the file they lead, not by a spreadsheet of logins.

Modules in this group
  • Clients. Client type, contact, address, point of contact, relationships, case types, the matters open for them, and the next date on any of them.
  • Team Chat. A whole-firm channel and direct messages inside the docket, with notifications running app-wide whichever view is open.
  • Notifications. Every docket action emits a notice to the firm-wide feed: opened files, diary entries, hearing updates, uploads, edits, deletions. The bell shows what you have not seen.
  • Users, Roles & Permissions. Super Admin, Partner, Senior Associate, Principal Associate and Associate. Everyone reads the whole book; editing a file's details, hearings, documents or diary is for its partner or team leader; the super admin can do everything.
  • Firm Settings & Workspaces. Firm name, address, counsel, enrolment, contact and logo, stamped on every draft; each user's profile and designation; sign-ins and the workspace each one owns; appearance, light and dark; automations that keep the docket current.
  • AI Usage & Cost. Settings → AI usage picks the tier every AI feature runs on. Balanced, the default, runs research, intake and the copilot on Claude Sonnet 5; Best runs them on Claude Opus 5 at roughly five times the price. Scanned pages are transcribed on the same model in either tier and cached by content, so a document is never paid for twice. Every research answer shows what it cost.

03 · Practice playbooks

The actions each practice reaches for, one click from Home.

Quick actions per practice area, grouped the way the work is done. Every playbook can send the file to Research; the Intellectual Property playbook hands it straight to the Trademark Suite's agents.

Civil

Filing to execution

Filing: draft plaint or petition, e-filing checklist, court fee calculator, vakalatnama pack. Hearings: add next date, cause list check, adjournment request, brief for counsel. Interim: application under Order 39, reply to injunction, status quo tracker. Evidence: affidavit of evidence, list of documents, cross-examination notes. Execution: execution petition, attachment tracker, warrant status. Client: status report email, fee note, matter opening form.

Criminal

Bail to compliance

Bail and custody: bail application, custody diary, surety verification, bond compliance. Trial: charge sheet analysis, cross-examination prep, witness schedule, Section 313 statement. Petitions: quashing, revision or appeal, anticipatory bail. Registry: certified copy request, case status on NJDG, FIR and annexures. Client: family update note, court date reminder, undertaking draft. Compliance: attendance exemption, bail condition tracker, limitation for appeal.

Family

Petitions to settlement

Petitions: divorce, maintenance under Section 125, custody, restitution. Mediation: mediation brief, settlement matrix, consent terms draft. Interim: interim maintenance, visitation schedule, protection order. Financials: income affidavit, asset disclosure, arrears calculator. Evidence: witness list, documents index, chat and call records. Client: sensitive-matter note, hearing reminder, status update.

Intellectual Property

Searches to enforcement

Searches: availability search, dilution matrix, watch notice, portfolio export. Prosecution: examination report response, written submission, prosecution history, renewal calendar. Opposition: notice of opposition, counter statement, evidence under Rule 45, review and appeal. Enforcement: cease and desist, John Doe application, online takedown, local commissioner. Advisory: chances of success, client mail, new filing advisory. Paralegal: hearing or advertisement, corrigenda, advertisement discrepancy. Each opens the matching agent in the Trademark Suite.
