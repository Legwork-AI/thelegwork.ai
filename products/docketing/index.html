02 · Every module, in detail

What each one holds, what you do in it, and what follows.

The modules work in five groups that share one register. Each group below is working in the docket today at ipmanager.thelegwork.ai.

Group 01 · The register
01–07

The register

IP files, filing rules and derived dates, Quality Control, E-files, connected files, clients and Global Search.

A file opens as the thing it is: a trade mark filing, a patent PCT, a design cancellation, a domain dispute. Its form carries the fields that procedure asks for and nothing else. Enter the examination report and the reply deadline is derived; enter the journal and the opposition window is; enter the filing and the renewal, surcharge and restoration dates are. Save a file in breach of its own rules and the save is refused with the reason. From then on Quality Control reads it every time it changes, and the next file in the chain opens pre-filled from this one.

Modules in this group
  • IP Files. Thirty-one file types across trade marks, patents, copyrights, designs, domains, geographical indications and IP litigation. Every file names its client, applicant, lawyer, partner and paralegal, carries its stage, its diaries and its connected files, and is listed in a register you filter by country, lawyer, client, status, class, mark type, diary date and portal watch.
  • Filing Rules & Derived Dates. Trade mark filings derive the one-month reply deadline, the four-month opposition window, the renewal date with its opening, surcharge and restoration dates, and the priority-document due date. Oppositions derive the whole statutory timetable: counter-statement, Rules 45, 46 and 47, the intention to appear, the three-month appeal. Appeals derive and lock their limitation date. Dozens of hard rules refuse a save that would breach the procedure; soft warnings flag what merely looks wrong, such as goods that read as another class or a reply filed late.
  • Quality Control. A fixed set of checks per file type, run live over the whole docket: a reply or appeal clock near or passed, a renewal window missed, a missing TM-48, Form 26 or Form 21 or an unsigned copy, a stage the portal contradicts, an adverse portal result, a duplicate filing, an identical mark for another client, a cited mark owned by a client, a file with no live diary. Each finding carries a severity; fix it, or sign it off and it stays signed off.
  • E-files. A folder tree per file type: Forum (application, examination report, reply and evidence, hearing, journal, opposition, certificate, renewal, recordals), Client (instructions, affidavits, reports) and Agent. PDF, Word, Excel, CSV and images, previewed in place. Documents live on cloud object storage under the firm's own prefix; the records live in a database scoped to the firm.
  • Connected Files. Open the filing from a cleared search; open a conflict from each mark cited in the examination report; open the opposition from a watch notice once the client says oppose; open the appeal from an adverse order; open a Madrid application on a basic mark; record an opposition's outcome on the opposed application. Each target is pre-filled, and the two files link on save. Files that share an application number connect by themselves.
  • Clients. Identity, address, billing, relationship, reporting preferences and contacts, a searchable list, and a detail page listing every matter for the client. The + beside Client on any form opens the same record in a modal.
  • Global Search. One index over every file. Words, exact phrases, exclusions, and fields: client:, lawyer:, partner:, stage:, country:, watch:. Overdue, due today, next 30 days are read as deadline filters, not text.

Group 02 · Dates
08–12

Dates

Dashboard, calendar, Diary Management, tasks and Workload Review.

Every dated field on a file is somebody's diary. The lawyer's dates go to the lawyer, the partner's to the partner, the paralegal's to the paralegal; the hard diary and every statutory or court date sit with the Super Admin, who sees all of them. The same date appears on the dashboard, the calendar, the person's diary page and the firm's workload review without being entered twice, and a date that is missed is flagged until it is dealt with.

Modules in this group
  • Dashboard. Your workload: the diaries due over the next seven days, grouped by day. The practice areas with live counts of active applications, registered marks, oppositions pending and renewals due. Recent activity across the firm, and the monthly filings.
  • Calendar. A month view of every deadline on the docket. Any entry opens its file.
  • Diary Management. Each person's own diary: today, the next three days, a week, two weeks, a month or any number of days, with overdue entries always included and flagged. Date, action, file and type; a row opens the file. Anyone can add a diary to any file; the tier is fixed by role; the entry is written to the file's history. Diaries are never edited or deleted.
  • Tasks. Anyone senior assigns work to anyone junior. Three columns: To do, Delegated, Marked done. A card carries its deadline stripe (red within two days, yellow within five, green beyond), attachments from either side, and a flow of open, done, completed or re-assigned with a new deadline. Every step notifies the other person.
  • Workload Review. For senior partners and above: one row per active account with open diaries counted as overdue, due today, within seven days, within thirty, and all open. Hard diaries and statutory dates on their own row; the firm totalled at the foot.

Group 03 · The registry, read for you
13–15

The registry, read for you

Portal watch, Fetch TMO records, and the TM-A filled from the file.

Nobody in the firm should spend a morning typing application numbers into ipindia.gov.in. The docket does it. On the schedule the firm sets, a private browser on the server signs in to the trade marks status portal or opens the Patent Office status page, answers the captcha itself, searches every number on the watch, reads the online status, downloads every document the registry has uploaded into the file's E-files, and compares what it found with the docket. What changed is written to the file's history and rung on the bell; what disagrees is flagged for Quality Control and never silently corrected.

Modules in this group
  • Portal Watch. Opt in per firm, every six hours to once a week, so many numbers a run, the most overdue first. What is on the watch is decided by the Super Admin's watch rules (any filter: practice area, module, stage, client, lawyer, class, portal status, filing dates, each with its own frequency) plus the files a person marked for watch, minus those a person excluded; the file page says which applies. Without a rule, pending files are checked every fourteen days and registered or granted ones every ninety. The trade marks portal's text puzzle is solved by rules and any image captcha is read by the model; the one-time password is typed into a prompt by whoever started the run, or arrives through an SMS webhook from the firm's portal mobile. A changed status, a new document or a new mismatch writes a history line, files the papers into E-files › Forum and notifies the lawyer, the partner and every Super Admin. A number that fails three runs is reported too. The top bar shows "Portal check · 3 of 12" while it runs.
  • Fetch TMO Records. The same flow for one file, now, from the button on the file or from Quality Control. The person who pressed it is asked for the OTP and told when it is done. Oppositions, conflicts and appeals fetch both sides' numbers in one login, and each side's status is compared with the matching mark and party.
  • TM-A E-filing. A desk brief on a trade mark filing with no application number yet checks the file against the portal's TM-A: the applicant's name, category and address, the mark and its type, at least one Nice class with its goods, the use claim and its affidavit, the Startup or MSME certificate for a reduced fee, the TM-48 marked filed with a signed copy in E-files, the address for service and the firm's e-filing login. What is missing is listed. When nothing is, File New TM-A signs in as the firm's attorney, reads the captcha, opens New Form Filing, chooses the applicant and application type, and fills every section from the file: applicant details, address for service, the mark, language, transliteration and translation, each class's goods, the statement as to use, other information and the verification. Every box is found by the label beside it, so one the portal has moved is reported under Left for you to fill rather than guessed. The desk shows each step, the log and a screenshot of the completed form, and the portal window stays open for two hours for you to review every section and press Save & Resume or Digitally Sign & Submit yourself.

Group 04 · Intelligence
16–18

Intelligence

Email Intelligence, the Paralegal Desk, and reports.

Instructions arrive by email and work is done by paralegals, and both used to leave no trace until someone typed them in. Email Intelligence reads the firm's mailbox for instructions and turns each one into a docket file on your say-so. The Paralegal Desk reads tomorrow's diaries and writes, for each one, what the file is, what the next step is and which documents it needs, then drafts them. Both run on the plan's credits; the rest of the docket does not.

Modules in this group
  • Email Intelligence. Connect Gmail or Outlook, read-only, one mailbox at a time. New mail is screened by a free keyword filter first; only likely instructions go to the model, several at a time, and each comes back as a yes/no card: what the client asks, which file type it is, what was extracted. Yes opens that module's form pre-filled from the email; on create, the whole thread is saved as a Word file with every real attachment beside it under E-files › Client. Cards sort into To review, Accepted, Dismissed and Set aside by AI, and any card can be flagged red, green, yellow or blue. The key never reaches the browser.
  • Paralegal Desk. Your own diaries (the lawyer's and team leader's tiers; never the hard, statutory or partner diaries) for tomorrow, the next two or three days, grouped by day. Tick them and press Run Desk: for each, the model reads the file's particulars, its open diaries, its Quality Control findings and the text of its latest E-file PDFs, and writes a three-to-four-line summary, one next step, the governing date and two to four suggested actions: an internal email, a client cover mail, a power of attorney on TM-48, a checklist, a follow-up letter to the Trade Marks Registry. Tick the actions, add your own, press Prepare, and each is drafted as a Word file kept on the file to view or download. A Registry letter is never free-drafted: the desk picks one of the firm's seven fixed TMR follow-up letters, fills the particulars from the record and the E-files, puts it on the firm's letterhead, and leaves blank any slot it cannot fill. A missing power of attorney is caught twice, by Quality Control and by the brief. A diary carries a yellow flag until briefed, then green.
  • Reports. Pick a source: any module, every file, the docket's dated entries, quality issues, E-files or clients. Filter on any field with AND/OR groups and relative periods; choose and order columns; sort; group by up to two fields with subtotals; pivot; chart. Seventeen templates to start from: renewals due, statutory deadlines this month, overdue diaries, examination replies, lawyer workload, opposition deadlines, Madrid renewals, client portfolio, high-risk conflicts, appeal limitation and more. Save, favourite, export to CSV or Excel, copy as a table, or print. Every row opens its file.
