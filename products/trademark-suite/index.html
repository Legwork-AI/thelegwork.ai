02 · Every agent, in detail

What each one reads, what it does, and what comes back.

The agents work in families that share one tool and one matter record. Each family below is working in the suite today. The inputs are the documents your desk already has. The output is a draft in the firm's format, ready for review.

Stage 01 · Clearance & search
01–02

Availability Search

Pre-filing clearance opinion for a proposed word mark, in the client's hands as a report.

Before a mark is filed, the client wants one answer: can we have it? The Availability Search reads a Registry search export for the proposed word mark, scores the absolute grounds, scores every cited mark on the relative grounds, runs a common law market search, and ends in a verdict the client can act on.

Key functions
  • Section 9 scored. Descriptive, generic and laudatory character assessed against the goods and services, with the reasoning stated.
  • Section 11 on every citation. Each cited mark scored on the firm's 24-parameter similarity framework: phonetic, visual, conceptual, goods, trade channels, consumer overlap.
  • Common law search. Unregistered use in the market surfaced alongside the register, so a prior user is not missed.
  • A verdict, not a list. Risk rating with the argument behind it, ending in Proceed, Reconsider or Abandon.
  • Client-ready report. The firm's search opinion format, not a raw export.
Reads
  • Proposed word mark, class and specification
  • Registry search Excel export
  • Client brief, if any
Returns
  • Availability / clearance search report
  • Proceed, Reconsider or Abandon recommendation

07

Dilution Matrix

How crowded is the register? Counted per class, per state, on one page.

A word carried by three hundred third-party marks is common to the trade. A word carried by three is worth protecting hard. The Dilution Matrix distils raw Registry exports into a class-by-class count of every third-party mark on the register, with the client's own group filings recognised and set aside. Every figure is counted from the register itself, never generated.

Key functions
  • Class × status grid. Every class searched, all 45 if need be, counted across Registered, Pending, Opposed and Invalid, with totals down both axes.
  • Counted, not generated. Deterministic code over the Registry's exports. The model never sees a number, so it cannot invent one.
  • Group-aware exclusion. Subsidiaries, national arms and spelling variants of the client recognised among proprietors and set aside.
  • Two views. The matrix excluding the client's own portfolio, and including it, side by side.
  • Registry-fluent parsing. Twenty-odd raw statuses normalised to four; multi-class placeholder rows resolved; duplicates across per-class exports collapsed.
  • A workbook, not a screenshot. Both summary matrices plus a drill-down sheet per status, every row carrying number, mark, proprietor and status.
Reads
  • Registry Excel exports, one per class
  • Or a finished Search Strings dilution sweep
  • The client's name and group
Returns
  • Excel workbook: two summary matrices
  • Detail sheet per register state

Notice, counter statement and the three evidence rounds, tracked as one sequence.

An opposition is five documents exchanged in a fixed order, each answering the last. The Opposition Drafter carries the sequence from the notice on Form TM-O through the counter statement to the Rule 45, 46 and 47 affidavits, on the same matter record, citing the firm's own earlier arguments, so nothing pleaded in one round is contradicted in the next.

Agents in this family

  • Rule 45 Evidence Affidavit. The opponent's evidence in support of the opposition: use, reputation, and the documents that prove them.
  • Rule 46 Evidence Affidavit. The applicant's evidence in support of the application.
  • Rule 47 Evidence Affidavit. The opponent's evidence in reply, confined to answering the applicant's evidence.

Prosecution runs on nudges: the letter that asks the Registrar to advertise the mark, correct the record, or issue the certificate. This tool keeps the firm's seven follow-ups as fixed templates. Pick the letter, type the particulars, and the finished Word file comes back on letterhead, ready for a signature. Nothing to review but the facts you typed.

The seven letters
  • Hearing / advertisement. Asks the Registrar to accept the mark for advertisement, or in the alternative appoint a hearing, once the objections stand answered.
  • Re-advertisement. Reports substantial errors in the published advertisement and asks for the mark to be re-advertised.
  • Discrepancy in advertisement. Reports typographical errors and asks for the records to be corrected.
  • Corrigenda. Asks for a corrigenda in the upcoming journal.
  • End of opposition. Confirms the opposition period lapsed unopposed and asks for the registration certificate to issue.
  • Discrepancy in registration certificate. Reports errors on the certificate issued.
  • Post registration. Lists the pending TM-M and TM-R requests and asks the Registrar to allow them.
Reads
  • Typed particulars only: reference number, letter date, application or registration number
  • Journal number and date, error list or pending requests, where the letter needs them
Returns
  • A .docx on firm letterhead, dated and signature-ready
  • A covering note naming anything left blank
