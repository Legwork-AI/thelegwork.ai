Group 01 · The file room
01–06

The file room

Case Files, the matter file, New Matter, Documents, Clients and Global Search.

A matter opens as the thing it is: a civil suit, a bail application, a maintenance petition, an infringement suit. Its file carries the parties, the forum and the bench, the case type and number, the CNR, the fee earner and the stage; its order sheet carries every hearing and what the court did at it; its folders carry the pleadings, the orders and the evidence. The next date on the order sheet is the date on the cause list, the calendar and the dashboard, without being entered twice.

Modules in this group
  • Case Files. The register of the firm's matters, with case-type tabs and folders, filters for status, fee earner and forum, a search across number, party, court and stage, and a sortable table or a card layout. Every row opens the matter.
  • The Matter File. The drawer that opens on any matter: particulars, the order sheet with each hearing and its outcome, the documents by folder, the tasks on the file and the history of every change. Upload a document from the drawer and it lands in the right folder; the file's CNR connects it to the court's own record.
  • New Matter. Two ways in. Fill the form, with the court, the case type and the parties picked from the firm's lists; or hand the intake the petition, the notice or the order and let the model read the parties, the forum, the number and the dates out of it into the form, for you to confirm before the matter is created. The file opens with its folder tree ready.
  • Documents. The firm's papers by case type and by matter, with the counts per folder and the recently filed list. PDF, Word, Excel and images previewed in place; scanned pages transcribed once and cached. Bytes live on cloud object storage under the firm's own prefix; the book itself never carries document contents.
  • Clients. The client book with the matters open for each client and the next date on every one.
  • Global Search. The search on the home screen reads a file number, a party, a court or a stage, and opens the file room on the matches.

Group 03 · The courts, read for you
11

The courts, read for you

Check Case Status, with the orders filed into the matter.

Nobody in the firm should spend the morning typing case numbers into court websites. From a matter, or from the status page, the docket asks the court itself: the Supreme Court, the High Courts, the district courts and the consumer commissions, by case number and court or by CNR. The status, the listing history and the orders come back onto the file, and the court's order PDFs are filed into the matter's orders folder, so the order sheet and the papers keep up with the court.

What it does
  • By number, by court. Pick the court the matter is in and give its number and year, or its CNR. The result is the court's own record: the parties, the status, the next listing, the bench, and the orders with their dates.
  • Orders, filed. Each order the court publishes is listed on the matter and can be opened or saved; new orders are filed into the matter's Court Orders folder without being downloaded by hand. An order the court withholds is shown as withheld, never fetched round the back.
  • Captchas, yours to solve. Where a court's website asks for a captcha, the docket shows it to you and waits; it never tries to defeat one. The Delhi High Court and the courts reached through the eCourts services need none.
  • Kept on the file. Every check is recorded on the matter with its time and result, so the next person opening the file sees what the court said last.

Group 04 · Intelligence
12–14

Intelligence

Research, Document Drop, and Ask Legwork.

Three places in the docket where a model does the reading. Research answers a question of law from the judgments and shows its sources. Document Drop reads a paper you give it and says which matter and which folder it belongs to. Ask Legwork answers questions about the book from the book. Each runs on the plan's own credits; the rest of the docket does not.

Modules in this group
  • Research. Ask in plain language: the limitation for an appeal against a decree in execution, the test for interim relief in a passing-off suit, what the Supreme Court said last on bail after charge-sheet. The answer is written from the judgments of the Supreme Court and the High Courts in the library, each proposition cited to its judgment with a link to the source PDF. Sessions keep their history; a long answer keeps running while you work elsewhere, and every answer shows what it cost.
  • Document Drop. Drop a PDF, Word or Excel file anywhere on the docket. The model reads it, names the matter it belongs to from the parties and the number, picks the folder, and shows you the filing it proposes; you confirm, and it is filed with its date. A paper for a matter not yet on the book opens New Matter with the intake pre-filled from it.
  • Ask Legwork. The copilot over the whole book: what is listed on a date, which matters are reserved for orders, what happened last on a file, which limitations run out this month, which of a client's matters have no next date. Every answer is taken from the record; it never invents a date.

03 · How a day moves

Filed once. Listed, checked, answered.

The book is kept by the people who work the matters, and it repays them by drawing the board, asking the court and reading the papers.

01OpenThe petition is dropped on the docket. The intake reads the parties, the forum and the number into the form; one confirmation and the matter is on the book with its folders.
02ListThe next date on the order sheet puts the matter on the cause list, the calendar and the dashboard. The fee earner's board for the morning is ready.
03CheckCheck Case Status asks the court. The order is filed into the matter, the next listing is on the file, and the task that follows carries its limitation.
04ResearchThe question of law is asked in plain language and answered from the judgments, each point cited with the PDF beside it.
05ReportWhat is listed this week, what is overdue, who carries what: any cut of the live book, for the partners' meeting.
