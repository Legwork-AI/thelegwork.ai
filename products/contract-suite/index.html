02 · Every tool, in detail

What each one reads, what it does, and what comes back.

The three tools share one agreement. A draft moves to review in a click, a review's accepted changes become the next version, and a clause worth keeping goes to the library from either.

Tool 01 · Drafting
01–10

Contract Drafting

Choose the contract, then upload the papers or fill in the details.

A client sends a term sheet, an email thread and last year's agreement, and wants the new one by evening. Choose the contract, upload what they sent, and the agreement is drafted from it. Or complete the contract's own form by hand. Either way the draft opens in the Draft Studio, in the firm's wording, with every gap marked.

Key functions
  • Two ways in, for every contract. Upload documents and the form is filled from them out of sight, or fill in the details yourself. After an upload the form shows what was read, to check, correct and draft again as the next version.
  • A form built for that contract. A lease asks about the deposit and the lock-in; a trademark licence asks about territory, quality control and royalty. Ten of the most-used agreements assemble from a fixed clause bank behind a detailed intake form; every other contract is drafted to its own clause list.
  • Which party, and what position. Name your client and choose to favour them, stay balanced, or accommodate the other side. The draft takes the matching position from the firm's playbook.
  • The firm's clauses, on request. Choose clauses from the Clause Library beside your instructions and the agreement is written with them.
  • From the chat as well. Ask the Co-Pilot for an NDA or a distribution agreement and the same drafting runs there, without opening the page.
  • On to review. A finished draft moves to Contract Review, and its clauses can be saved to the library.

  • Your instructions, and any clauses chosen from the library

Tool 02 · Review
R1–R4

Contract Review

Every risk as a redline, with the reason, the next step and the authority.

The other side has sent their paper, or your own draft needs a second read before it goes. The agreement is reviewed from your client's side against the firm's playbook and the review standard for that kind of contract. Every critical, high and medium risk comes back as a redline you accept or reject.

Key functions
  • The agreement, redlined. Struck and inserted text drawn into the agreement and numbered, beside a card for each change: why it is a risk, what to do next, and on what authority.
  • Or as a table. The same redlines one row each: clause, risk, existing wording, proposed wording, reason, next action, authority.
  • Graded by risk. Critical, high and medium, with a filter, so the review can be read from the top down.
  • Statutes checked against the bare act. A section the review cites is looked up in central legislation before you see it. Found, it comes with the section's own heading, its text and the source page. Not found, the page says so.
  • Accept, reject, add. Decide each redline. Add a clause from the library as an insertion, numbered to fit.
  • Out as tracked changes. What you did not reject leaves as a Word file with tracked changes over the agreement as it came in, or goes back to the Draft Studio as the next version.
Reads
  • The draft from Contract Drafting, or an uploaded agreement in Word, PDF or text
  • Which party is your client, and the position to take
  • The firm's playbook, and your context
Returns
  • The redlined agreement and the redline table
  • A found or not-found mark on every statute cited
  • A Word file with tracked changes

A flag, not an opinion. A point of law in a review is there for the lawyer to confirm. The redlines are proposals, and nothing is changed in the agreement until you accept it.

Tool 03 · Clause Library
C1–C3

Clause Library

What the firm has settled, kept once and used everywhere.

Every firm has a limitation of liability clause a partner fought for, and three associates who each retype a different one. The Clause Library keeps the firm's clauses in one place, filed under plain headings, and gives them to the drafting and the review.

Key functions
  • Filed under headings. Confidentiality, Intellectual Property, Fees and Payment, Limitation of Liability and the rest, with any heading of your own added to the list.
  • Saved out of an agreement. A draft, a reviewed agreement or an uploaded precedent is read into its clauses; tick the ones to keep.
  • Or typed in. Add and edit a clause directly, marked for the kind of contract it was written for, or for any.
  • Search by heading or wording. Find the clause by what it is called or by a phrase in it.
  • Shared across the firm. One library for everyone, with who added each clause and where it came from.

03 · Capabilities

Built against the ways contract work goes wrong.

A supplier's cap lands in a customer's contract. A redline cites a section that says something else. The clause the partner approved last month is retyped from memory. The suite is built against exactly those failures.

Drafting

From the client's own papers

The agreement is drafted from the documents on the matter, and whatever they do not establish stays a marked blank.

Position

The right side of the deal

Client-favouring, balanced or accommodating, chosen per matter, so a supplier's position is never applied to a customer.

Playbook

Your positions, written down

Preferred, balanced, acceptable fallback and never accept, for each clause that matters. Change the playbook and every draft and review follows.

Review

Redlines graded by risk

Critical, high and medium, each with the reason and the next action, in the agreement or as a table.

Authority

Citations checked, not trusted

Statutes are looked up in the bare act and shown with the section's text, or marked not found.

Consistency

One clause, one wording

The library carries the firm's settled wording into every draft and every review that needs it.
